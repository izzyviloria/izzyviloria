About Me

I am a Neuroscience student at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill who has a strong interest in the intersection of medicine, neuroscience, and biomedical innovation. Driven by a lifelong curiosity about how complex systems function, I am particularly fascinated by the brain and the emerging technologies that can restore and improve human health.

My interest in neuroscience is both academic and personal. As someone who experiences migraines, I have developed a deep appreciation for the complexity of neurological disorders and the need for continued research into their causes and treatments. Through my coursework and research interests, I hope to better understand the mechanisms underlying brain function and contribute to advancements in neurological care.

Beyond neuroscience, I am especially interested in biomedical technologies and regenerative medicine. Innovations such as skin grafts, tissue engineering, and other grafting techniques demonstrate how scientific research can translate into life-changing treatments for patients. I am drawn to the challenge of developing solutions that repair damaged tissues, restore function, and improve quality of life.

Through my experiences in science coursework, clinical shadowing, and volunteer service, I have developed a passion for combining research, technology, and patient care. My long-term goal is to contribute to medical innovation as a physician and researcher, helping bridge the gap between scientific discovery and meaningful patient outcomes.
